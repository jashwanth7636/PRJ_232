AI-Powered Maintenance Chatbot for Electrical Substations

An industrial-grade SCADA decision-support platform integrating supervised ensemble machine learning for 3-phase fault classification with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for equipment maintenance procedures.

Project Purpose & Motivation
Addressing critical challenges in high-voltage substation maintenance
⚡

Electrical transmission and distribution substations are vital nodes in modern power grids. Operating at voltage levels ranging from 33kV to 765kV, switchgear and transformers are subject to severe electrical, thermal, and mechanical stresses. Unplanned outages result in massive economic losses and pose severe safety hazards to substation operators.

Traditional substation maintenance relies heavily on static physical manuals and retrospective fault logs. When abnormal relay flags or tripping events occur, field technicians often lack instantaneous access to contextual diagnosis and certified standard operating procedures.

PRJ_232 bridges this gap by offering a dual-engine architecture: (1) An ultra-fast, offline-capable Random Forest fault classifier that evaluates raw 3-phase current and voltage waveforms to pinpoint fault topology in milliseconds; and (2) A Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) copilot powered by Gemini Flash that searches 884+ indexed sections of official standards (IS 10028, IEEE C37, NEMA AB 4) to guide operators through safe remediation.

Supervised Machine Learning: Model Evaluation & Benchmarks
Empirical model selection based on stratified test split metrics

RAG Architecture & Document Retrieval Pipeline
Retrieval-Augmented Generation for strict engineering grounding
🧠
1. Ingestion & Chunking
Technical standards in PDF format are processed via pypdf. Text streams are segmented into overlapping 1,200-character windows with a 200-character stride to maintain semantic coherence across section breaks.
2. Vector Space & Retrieval
All 884+ segments are represented as high-dimensional TF-IDF vectors. Incoming user questions are vectorized and ranked via cosine similarity to extract the top-4 most relevant procedural excerpts.
3. Generative Synthesis
Retrieved chunks are passed as strict context to Google Gemini Flash with temperature set to 0.2 and 2,500 token headroom, enforcing structured markdown output and exact document citations.
